The same companies, one quarter later: claims persist, with revenue claims the exception.

Of the 470 Q1 adopters that have reported Q2, 72% repeated the AI claim, and the claims are sticky in kind: efficiency stayed efficiency (29 companies), product stayed product (7). Nine companies upgraded efficiency → revenue. 12 went quiet entirely.

The exception is the claim investors care most about. Of 46 Q1 revenue claimants reporting Q2, only 23 still describe AI driving revenue, and just 14 back it with a number. The revenue story is the least durable claim in the dataset.

Disclosure is rising (35% → 44% of reporters) while quantification is flat (25% → 24%). More companies are telling the story; the share proving it hasn't moved. The Q1 market test found the same from the other side: AI disclosure carried no return premium (+0.20pp vs sector, p=0.77) and no employment-substitution signal. AI and margins: the stated link is rare.

Margins are a central focus this season, so we asked every AI disclosure a second question: does the company explicitly connect its AI use to its own margins, in a verbatim, transcript-verified statement? Most don’t. Of 605 adopters, 41 make the connection on the record, 16 attach a number, and of those only 13 describe realized results rather than future targets. Efficiency talk is everywhere; the stated margin link is rare, and the quantified, delivered version is rarer still.

Method. Every S&P 1500 earnings transcript is read end-to-end and screened for a description of the company using AI; each entry carries a verbatim quote programmatically verified against the transcript; no verifiable quote, no entry. Companies that only sell AI to others are excluded; hybrids classify by internal use. Extraction identical across both seasons. Q1 window: April 1 – July 15; Sweep window: July 15 – October 21, 2026 (Q3 2026 season in progress; coverage grows as transcripts post). Extraction is re-run each refresh; borderline vendor/product hybrids can flip between runs (~4% of the universe); headline findings sit well clear of that noise.

Absence is not evidence. This measures disclosure, not deployment. Metrics are management's own, unaudited. Not investment advice.
