Do the claims persist?

Of the 236 companies that claimed AI use earlier in 2026 and have reported Q3, 58% repeated the claim on their Q3 call; 99 went quiet. Claims are sticky in kind (efficiency stayed efficiency for 65, product for 32; 5 upgraded to a revenue claim). The revenue story remains the rarest: of 18 prior revenue claimants reporting Q3, 9 still describe AI driving revenue and 5 back it with a number. Recomputed at every refresh as Q3 transcripts post.

AI and margins: the stated link is rare.

Margins are a central focus this season, so we asked every AI disclosure a second question: does the company explicitly connect its AI use to its own margins, in a verbatim, transcript-verified statement? Most don’t. Of 538 adopters, 36 make the connection on the record, 16 attach a number, and of those only 16 describe realized results rather than future targets. Efficiency talk is everywhere; the stated margin link is rare, and the quantified, delivered version is rarer still.

Method. Every S&P 1500 earnings transcript is read end-to-end and screened for a description of the company using AI; each entry carries a verbatim quote programmatically verified against the transcript; no verifiable quote, no entry. Companies that only sell AI to others are excluded; hybrids classify by internal use. Extraction identical across both seasons. Q1 window: April 1 – July 15; Sweep window: July 15 – October 30, 2026 (Q3 2026 season in progress; coverage grows as transcripts post). Extraction is re-run each refresh; borderline vendor/product hybrids can flip between runs (~4% of the universe); headline findings sit well clear of that noise.

Absence is not evidence. This measures disclosure, not deployment. Metrics are management's own, unaudited. Not investment advice.
