The same companies, one quarter later: claims persist. Revenue claims don't.

Of the 344 Q1 adopters that have reported Q2, 72% repeated the AI claim — and the claims are sticky in kind: efficiency stayed efficiency (125 companies), product stayed product (56). Nine companies upgraded efficiency → revenue. 98 went quiet entirely.

The exception is the claim investors care most about. Of 26 Q1 revenue claimants reporting Q2, only 14 still describe AI driving revenue — and just 6 back it with a number. The revenue story is the least durable claim in the dataset.

Disclosure is rising (35% → 44% of reporters) while quantification is flat (25% → 24%). More companies are telling the story; the share proving it hasn't moved. The Q1 market test found the same from the other side: AI disclosure carried no return premium (+0.20pp vs sector, p=0.77) and no employment-substitution signal. Method. Every S&P 1500 earnings transcript is read end-to-end and screened for a description of the company using AI; each entry carries a verbatim quote programmatically verified against the transcript — no verifiable quote, no entry. Companies that only sell AI to others are excluded; hybrids classify by internal use. Extraction identical across both seasons. Q1 window: April 1 – July 15; Q2 window: July 15 – August 20, 2026 (Q2 season in progress — the Aug 19–21 retail wave and NVDA are pending). Extraction is re-run each refresh; borderline vendor/product hybrids can flip between runs (~4% of the universe) — marquee findings sit well clear of that noise.

Absence is not evidence. This measures disclosure, not deployment. Metrics are management's own, unaudited. Not investment advice.
