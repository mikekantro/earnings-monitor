The same companies, one quarter later: claims persist, with revenue claims the exception.

Of the 346 Q1 adopters that have reported Q2, 71% repeated the AI claim, and the claims are sticky in kind: efficiency stayed efficiency (123 companies), product stayed product (57). Nine companies upgraded efficiency → revenue. 99 went quiet entirely.

The exception is the claim investors care most about. Of 27 Q1 revenue claimants reporting Q2, only 14 still describe AI driving revenue, and just 8 back it with a number. The revenue story is the least durable claim in the dataset.

Disclosure is rising (35% → 44% of reporters) while quantification is flat (25% → 24%). More companies are telling the story; the share proving it hasn't moved. The Q1 market test found the same from the other side: AI disclosure carried no return premium (+0.20pp vs sector, p=0.77) and no employment-substitution signal. AI and margins: the stated link is rare.

Margins are a central focus this season, so we asked every AI disclosure a second question: does the company explicitly connect its AI use to its own margins, in a verbatim, transcript-verified statement? Most don’t. Of 410 adopters, 24 make the connection on the record, 7 attach a number, and of those only 4 describe realized results rather than future targets. Efficiency talk is everywhere; the stated margin link is rare, and the quantified, delivered version is rarer still.

Method. Every S&P 1500 earnings transcript is read end-to-end and screened for a description of the company using AI; each entry carries a verbatim quote programmatically verified against the transcript; no verifiable quote, no entry. Companies that only sell AI to others are excluded; hybrids classify by internal use. Extraction identical across both seasons. Q1 window: April 1 – July 15; Q2 window: July 15 – August 23, 2026 (Q2 season in progress; the Aug 19–21 retail wave and NVDA are pending). Extraction is re-run each refresh; borderline vendor/product hybrids can flip between runs (~4% of the universe); headline findings sit well clear of that noise.

Absence is not evidence. This measures disclosure, not deployment. Metrics are management's own, unaudited. Not investment advice.
