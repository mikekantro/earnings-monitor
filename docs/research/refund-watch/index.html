Refund retention and pricing: a result under review.

When first published (data to Aug 6), refund retainers’ pricing signal had firmed +2.35 points versus sector quarter-over-quarter (p=0.003), clearing our multiple-testing bar. On refreshes since, the retain-vs-control effect has attenuated (+1.71, +1.78, now +1.55, p=0.042), below the p<0.008 bar. But the committed retest is arriving: with Target’s $994M price investment joining the pass-through group, that cohort swung to −1.14, consistent with price investment, widening the retain-vs-pass-through spread to +2.47 (n=14, p=0.23, still underpowered). Walmart and Costco complete the cohort this week.

The supporting structure held: the pass-through group still moved the opposite way (−0.15), retainers’ overall PMI is no different from anyone else’s, and the retained pool grew to $3.3 billion. But our standard is the standard: attenuation on refresh is what fragile findings do, and we treat this as a hypothesis on watch, not a finding, until the late-August retail wave re-tests it with the largest pass-through cohort of the season.

Method & discipline. Sector-neutral quarter-over-quarter change in the PMI prices sub-index; permutation test, 30,000 draws. Our pre-committed rule is that findings below the corrected significance bar are labeled as hypotheses, not results; we show the attenuation across refreshes rather than quoting the stronger print. Permanent caveat: the prices sub-index reads pricing power and input costs from the same call; the pass-through group’s opposite move argues against a mechanical artifact but at n=13 cannot settle it. Retail prints so far: HD retained $685M; LOW retained $80M; TGT invested $994M in price. WMT and COST complete the retest this week.

Method. Every S&P 1500 company reporting July 15 – October 22, 2026 was screened for tariff-refund discussion. Claude reads each FactSet transcript and classifies direction (receiving from the government vs. refunding customers) and stated disposition, with a verbatim supporting quote programmatically verified against the transcript; entries without a verifiable quote are dropped, not guessed. Amounts are as stated by management; the dollar bar buckets each company's full disclosed amount by its primary stated disposition, an approximation where plans are split (Amazon's $600M is partly customer refunds, partly price investment). Explicitly quantified customer refunds remain roughly $38M across the whole dataset (Mettler-Toledo $28M, Hayward ~$9M, PHINIA ~$1M); the $0.7B customer figure is therefore a ceiling, not a commitment.

Coverage caveat. The heaviest refund-disposition cohort, big-box retail, reports late August: Walmart, Target, Costco, Deere and others are not yet in this data. The disposition mix may shift when they land. NVDA and other late reporters likewise pending.

Legal caveat. Some customer-refund commitments arise amid litigation over how import charges were passed through (a proposed class action concerning Amazon's handling is pending). "Will refund customers" can be legal posture as much as capital allocation; this page records what was said, not what will be paid.

Limits. Company-reported, unaudited figures; received vs. expected amounts are mixed as stated. Descriptive, not predictive. Not investment advice.
