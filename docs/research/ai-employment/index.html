Are AI adopters lagging the hiring recovery?

Companies that never mention AI are hiring into an improving market. Companies with a persistent AI claim are not. This page tracks that gap under a pre-committed significance bar and a pre-committed decision date, until the data promotes it to a finding or retires it.

Results by claim intensity

What keeps this hypothesis alive is the stability of the core gap: across five prints and two independent extraction runs, persistent AI claimants sit 0.6–0.8 points below never-claimants (p=0.03–0.08), while first-time claimants track the baseline. A dose-response ordering appeared in earlier refreshes, and we reported it, but its deepest tier broke on re-extraction this week (see the table note), so the ordering is no longer part of the evidence. The claim now rests on the persistent-vs-never gap and the operate-vs-sell sector split, nothing more. Ordering was never significance, and the bar is the bar.

Methodology and pre-commitments

Pre-committed, in writing, before the answer is known

The bar. Promotion to a finding requires the headline cut to clear p<0.006 (Bonferroni-corrected for the eight tests run each refresh). p in the 0.03–0.06 range across refreshes does not qualify, however suggestive the pattern.

The decision date. Q3 2026 earnings season roughly doubles the persistent-claimant panel, pushing the minimum detectable effect below the currently observed one. That run, not this one, decides. The hypothesis log, fixed random seed, and test battery live in a version-controlled script so the Q3 numbers cannot be quietly re-specified.

The exit. If Q3 comes back null, this page reports the retirement with the same prominence as a confirmation.

Method. Employment is one of six sub-indices in our earnings-call PMI, scored 0–100 under a fixed rubric across 1,432 Q1 and 907 Q2 S&P 1500 companies. AI claimants are identified by verbatim, transcript-verified disclosure extraction. All comparisons are sector-neutral; significance by permutation test (30,000 draws, fixed seed).

Caveats that travel with every number here. Shared source: the employment score and the AI claim come from the same call, so a CEO saying “AI lets us grow without adding headcount” contributes to both sides; the panel design mitigates but cannot eliminate this. Small tiers are additionally extraction-sensitive: the quantified-both-seasons tier (n≈12–16) changes membership between model extraction runs, and its print swung from −1.8 to −0.1 on re-extraction this week; it is shown for completeness, not evidence. The headline tier (n=245) is stable across all runs. Disclosure is not deployment: quiet adopters sit in the control group and bias against the effect. Descriptive, not causal. Not investment advice.
