The decomposition

The index improved this quarter. The bars split that improvement, exactly, into the part contributed by build-out claimants and the part contributed by everyone else, weighted by cohort size. On new orders, where backlogs live, the concentration is starker.

So the straight comparison: the 96 build-out claimants that have re-reported moved +0.21 on average, everyone else (359 companies) -1.43 — a cohort gap of +1.64 points. In Q2’s full season the cohort contributed roughly half the index’s improvement; whether that premium reappears is the thing to watch as the panel fills. New-orders cut: cohort -0.06 vs -2.40.

What this is. An arithmetic decomposition: the index’s average change splits exactly into cohort contributions weighted by size. Membership requires management explicitly linking AI-infrastructure demand to the company’s own revenue, orders, backlog, or outlook, with a verbatim quote; no quote, no entry. Companies merely using AI internally are tracked on the AI Adoption tab, not here.

What this is not. Not a causal estimate. Second-order exposure (the freight carrier hauling transformers) is invisible unless stated, so the share is a floor on direct attribution. Self-attribution carries fashion risk: some companies may over-claim the theme, which the Q3 retest will measure. One check already runs against it: claimants without a stated figure improved +0.5, nearly matching the +0.0 of those with numbers, and both far exceed the -1.4 non-claimant baseline, so the cohort is not carried by numberless talk. Figures are equal-weighted; a capitalization-weighted cut would concentrate further.

Season note. Figures update with each refresh as late reporters post and are recomputed from the full matched sample each run.
