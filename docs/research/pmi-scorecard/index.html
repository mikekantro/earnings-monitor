How the scores are built

This is an ISM-style diffusion index derived from earnings calls rather than purchasing-manager surveys. Each company's quarterly call is read in full context and scored on six sub-indices under a fixed rubric applied identically to every company in both quarters. Each sub-index runs 0–100, where 50 divides expansion from contraction; a 62 is not “better” than a 58 by four grades; both say conditions are improving, one more emphatically.

Sub-indexWeightWhat it reads from the call
New orders30%Bookings, backlog, pipeline, demand commentary
Output25%Revenue delivery, production, utilization
Employment15%Hiring, headcount, labor availability and cost
Prices15%Pricing power, realized price, input costs
Supply chains10%Lead times, logistics, inventory, tariffs
Demand breadth5%How widespread demand is across segments and geographies

Reading the numbers. The composite is the weighted average of the six. Two interpretation notes that differ from the ISM you may be used to: prices contribute to the composite here (in ISM they are reported separately), so firming prices lift the headline; read the prices row on its own when thinking about inflation. And a sub-index above 50 means rising, not good: prices at 61 means pricing is firming, which cuts differently for margins than for the consumer.

Consistency over completeness. Every company is scored from the same fixed-length transcript window (prepared remarks and the opening of Q&A) applied uniformly, so scores are comparable across companies and across quarters. The trade-off is accepted deliberately: a consistent partial read beats an inconsistent full one for index construction.

Quarter-over-quarter discipline. All Q2-vs-Q1 figures on this page compare only the 5 companies scored in both seasons, against their own Q1 scores; changes in who has reported so far cannot masquerade as changes in conditions. Sector bars require at least 10 matched companies.

Source & scoring. FactSet earnings-call transcripts, scored by Claude (Anthropic) under a fixed rubric; see “How the scores are built” above. Key-signal excerpts are drawn from each company's own call.

Coverage. Q2: 6 companies scored through October 1, 2026; late reporters, retailers in particular and NVDA, will move these figures. Q1: 1,432 companies, full season.

Limits. The index measures the story management told, calibrated consistently, not audited results. It is descriptive, not predictive. Not investment advice.
